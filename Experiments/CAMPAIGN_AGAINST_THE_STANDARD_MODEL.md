## 0. The criterion (corrected)

> **The Standard Model is sunk wherever it cannot account for a result that SDT can.**

Not "wherever SDT forward-predicts something new." The SM was *built* by observation → interpretation →
fitting until it could predict; it never forward-predicted α, the masses, the mixing angles, the Higgs
mass, or g−2. Holding SDT to a pre-registration bar the winning theory never cleared is a purity test,
not a strategy. So the kill has **two legitimate faces**:

1. **Divergent fork** — SDT and the SM predict *different measurable values* for the same observable.
   Whoever the data lands on, the other is wrong. Cleanest kill; rarest.
2. **Explanatory kill** — the SM can only *absorb* a result (a free parameter, or a dark epicycle) that
   SDT *derives* or *dissolves* from structure. Reproducing an existing result the SM cannot account for,
   with fewer knobs, is a kill by this criterion — no novel prediction required.

**Fine-tuning is the method, not the sin.** The rule is the one already inside "fit until it predicts
accurately": fit a structural relationship to data, **label the fit a fit** (CALIBRATED, not DERIVED),
then drive it until it predicts a value it was *not* fitted to. That last clause is the whole discipline —
it is what separated the SM's coupling fits (which then gave the W and Z masses) from our own graveyard
of constants that fitted a famous number and stopped. Fit freely; certify honestly; validate out-of-sample.
