The Sun. A raindrop. An atom. Physics gives three answers — gravity, surface tension, the quantum — for one shape. Let's follow that coincidence until it breaks.

One · the coincidence

Everything, everywhere, is round.

The Sun, our planet, the Moon. A water droplet, a cell, its nucleus. The atom, the electron, the nuclear core — down to a dot so small it can't be any other shape. Thirty orders of magnitude, one answer every time: a ball.

Two · the crack

The Sun I get. The raindrop is where it breaks.

The Sun's gravity draws it inward from every side — fine. But a droplet has no gravity to speak of, so we reach for a different cause: surface tension. And the atom? A third: the quantum.

Three unrelated mechanisms, one identical shape, across thirty orders. That is not an explanation — it's a coincidence wearing three coats.

Three · hold on…

Why isn't the universe a field of spokes?

Gravity pulls harder up close — so the near side of an orbiting body is pulled harder than its far side. The same body, two pulls. Stretch that and everything becomes a spear pointing at what it circles.

The stretch is real — the tidal force, 2GMR/r³. Drag the moon inward and watch it lose to its primary.

Four · the sidestep

A pure pull is a machine for stretch and collapse.

Left alone, an inward 1/r² pull stretches a body and draws a cloud onto itself — the moon crumbles at the Roche limit, the star collapses when its fire dies, matter falls into a black hole as spaghetti. Only an outward push ever stops it.

General relativity doesn't remove this. It coordinates it — the pull becomes curvature, the collapse is predicted outright as the black hole. The scandal was quietly turned into geometry.

One shape, one collapse, one missing push — patched by a different hand each time.

Five · 1748

Le Sage said gravity was never a pull. It was a push.

Fill space with fast movers from every direction. A lone body is battered equally and goes nowhere — but two bodies each shadow the stream between them, and the unshadowed push from outside drives them together.

One mechanism. And it makes balls for free: an isotropic push has only one equilibrium shape.

Five · ruled out on two counts

Both objections were the answer.

Fatal flaw #1 · Thermalisation

The battering would cook a body white-hot.

And there it is — the molten core, the geothermal gradient with no clean source, the furnace of every star. Le Sage's "impossible" heat is the heat that is already there, unexplained.

Fatal flaw #2 · Drag

The stream would spiral the planets in.

Only if matter moves through a stream. Make it a medium matter displaces and drag evaporates: a bubble in water feels none — the water reorganises around it. That cost is inertia; its imbalance is gravity.

The phenomena that were meant to refute the push are the phenomena that require it.

Six · and yet…

Le Sage was still wrong.

His movers need little bullet, big sky — grains fine enough, a cosmos empty enough, to cross without forever colliding. And they must share that space with the other medium of the age: the aether that carried light, a wispy gas filling everything, which by its very office would have to let the fast, thermalising bullets pass straight through.

Two seas in one room — one thick enough to carry light, one thin enough to let the shot by. It does not close.

Close — and no cigar-shaped moons, no field of spokes. But no cigar.

Seven · then invert it

Take the properties of both — and neither of the substances.

A material that occupies space is only GR's arena renamed. So stop putting matter in space. Make it a bubble that excludes space — a knot that shoulders the lattice out and is crushed by it in return. The pull was never a pull; it is space, closing on the exclusion.

And the push is no hail of bullets. It is an influx — everywhere, all at once, the same at every point: one throughflow, carrier of light, force and energy alike.

Eight · four words

Spatial Displacement Theory.

The ball is the shape of anything held by an inward influx the same in every direction — Sun, droplet, electron, one pressure at thirty orders. Gravity is that influx, occluded; the "pull" is space closing on a displacement. Heat is that influx, processed. No G, no M — only how much of the sea a body excludes, and how far it stands.
