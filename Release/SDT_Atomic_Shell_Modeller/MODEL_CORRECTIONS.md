## Active physical inputs

The dynamic path uses only `z = kappa/r`, solid angle, `F_occlusion`, and torque from `Engine/include/sdt/laws.hpp` quantities. `v0 = c sqrt(kappa/r)` is an initial tangential speed, then the calculated tangential force integrates carrier phase via `domega/dt = F_tangent/(m ds/dphi)`. The finite lobe-tour radius remains `r_tour = kappa/z`.

## Grammar scope and natural catalogue

- The alpha/deuteron/triton algebra is explicitly scoped to `Z >= 3` by `docs/atomicus-nuclear-model.html`; applying it to `1H`, `2H`, or `3He` as though they were failed composites was an audit error.
- The natural-abundance filter of `ATOMICUS/tools/_iaea_ground_states.csv` selects `288` entries in the supplied extract. Its three light bases outside the composite rule are rendered directly: proton (`1H`), deuteron (`2H`), and helion mirror (`3He`). The triton primitive is `3H`, not `3He`.
- `4He` is the alpha base. The lithium constructions are `6Li = alpha + d` and `7Li = alpha + t`; the display makes their cap axis the lobe-axis initial condition.
- IAEA records supply observed identifiers and measured nuclear properties, not alpha/deuteron/triton positions, packing order, or electronic paths. The app now loads the positive IAEA LiveChart rms charge-radius record for each supported natural isotope as a packing-envelope cap. Those visual coordinates remain Atomicus construction inputs and must not be presented as an observational result.
- The supplied local lithium abundance values (`4.85%` and `95.15%`) conflict with NIST representative terrestrial compositions (`7.59(4)%` and `92.41(4)%`). The app overrides and labels the latter values. This correction does not alter the isotope membership or nuclear count ledger.
- Lithium is a null test for an electronic explanation: both neutral isotopes use the same `1s2 2s` occupation input, while measured nuclear moments differ in the opposite direction to a “Li-6 is more magnetic” claim (`6Li: +0.822056`, `7Li: +3.25644` nuclear magnetons). No magnetic cascade is claimed until its field, spectral, and power observables are specified and measured.
- Neutral Li/Be tour condition: the `2s` tour axis is drawn perpendicular to the displayed `1s` reference axis. Neutral Li keeps one post-`1s` oval. Be uses the maximin two-carrier construction: its apoapses are diametrically opposed and begin at matched apoapsis phase, while its two planes remain distinct. This is user-specified geometry, not an observational claim or a dynamic term.
- Carbon/Li/Be frame condition: carbon `1s2` has two polar great-ellipse tour planes sharing and crossing at the packing-derived poles. The renderer contains one atom at a time, so this is a declared frame convention rather than a measured inter-atomic relation. It does not determine post-`1s` apoapses.
- Atomic Shape Cycle / apoapsis condition: the supplied visual cycle remains an initial lobe-frame geometry, separate from SDT dynamics. Every post-`1s` carrier addition recomputes all apoapsis directions as a maximin spherical layout: two through six use exact antipodal, triangular, tetrahedral, triangular-bipyramidal, and octahedral constructions; larger sets use deterministic numerical relaxation. The result is an angular geometry condition, not a solved pressure equilibrium, directional resistance, magnetic response, density, or added force. The removed wireframe guides are not treated as physical objects.
- Four-nucleon contact/volume condition: alpha-containing displays expose the tetrahedral contact geometry and one poloidal reference loop. The contact volume is a declared scale proxy, not observational nuclear-density data. The audit correctly separates the existing fixed-separation `Omega, F_occlusion ∝ V^(2/3)` consequence from the current displacement law, which contains no volume term. No nucleon motion, contact force, or volume-displacement coupling has been inserted.
- D/T contact-torus condition: Law IV supplies displaced volumes and spherical exclusion radii. The `L=2 pi a0` back-filter widths close `V_disp` but are not wake or containment radii. `R_wake` is rotating-spation kinematics; containment uses the physical matter/contact boundary. Displacement cascades through shells to create the pressure boundary against matter. Direct light blocking is not assigned to the wake; only a separately demonstrated coherent flux may produce polarisation.
- Nuclear-radius constraint: `data/iaea-livechart-natural-charge-radii-2026-08-28.json` holds the local IAEA LiveChart extraction. It supplies positive experimental rms charge radii for `285` natural-isotope entries. `33S`, `50V`, and `231Pa` are deliberately excluded because the source reports radius `0` (unavailable), and no imputation or mass-law fallback is permitted. Current NIST/CODATA rms charge radii supersede the archived IAEA values for `1H` (`0.84075 fm`), `2H`, and `4He`. An rms charge radius is not a hard physical surface. The renderer therefore applies it only as the requested, explicit packing cap without converting it through an unmeasured density profile: it contracts the generated packing centres where needed, keeps all fixed component dimensions unchanged, and rejects a component that cannot fit. The scale factor and final envelope are displayed. This enforces a radius constraint but does not validate the generated positions, contact topology, or density distribution.
- Element-specific boundary / post-`1s` core-orbit condition: `K` uses the circular convention `k=c/v` and `K=r_atom/k²`. Hydrogen gives `v=alpha c`, `k=1/alpha`, and `K=alpha²a0=2.8179403204 fm=r_e=r_S/2`. The prior `sqrt(2)` conversion was an escape-speed substitution and doubled every K. The coupled relations remain `z=K/r`, `zk²=1`, and `v=c sqrt(K/r)`, hence `v(K)=c` with a declared superluminal interior.
- C-boundary rendering: the boundary is a fixed sphere. Its screen radius is calculated directly from fixed `K`, model display scale, viewport size, and user zoom. Structural rotation no longer projects a rotating edge vector, eliminating false foreshortening-driven radius oscillation.
- Electron-body dynamics: direct electron-electron occlusion and solid angle use the Law IV point-sized `R_excl,e`, not the geometric-mean charge radius. The latter remains a Coulomb-normalisation construction and is not treated as a literal collision cross-section. Wake-mediated coupling remains separate and is not silently substituted.
- Display-time condition: the selector multiplies only `viewSecondsToPhysicalSeconds`, allowing presentation slowdown without changing carrier speed, `z`, force, or any other equation.
- Playback/navigation condition: play and pause change only whether updates are applied. Single arrows select the adjacent natural-isotope record; double arrows select the nearest available neutral occupation closure (`Z = 2, 10, 18, 36, 54, 86`). This is catalogue navigation, not a newly inferred physical closure process.

## Open audit

The engine declares `r_atom=a0 n²/Z`; the selected element combines that radius with unmodified circular `k=c/v` to obtain `K=r_atom/k²`. Recoil is not folded into K because doing so would contradict the defining hydrogen identity `K=alpha²a0`; reduced-mass treatment remains a separate spectroscopic conversion. Blank-term NIST centroids are filtered, both proton-radius uses are unified at `0.8414 fm`, and the malformed f-lobe return is repaired.

The app has no NIST table of directly observed neutral-atom valence-electron velocities. The only NIST velocity relation used is the hydrogenic `alpha = v1/c` calibration. Non-hydrogen values are explicitly labelled first-ionization equivalent-speed inputs; treating them as direct NIST velocity observations would be false.
