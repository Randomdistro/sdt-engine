## 1. The flaw — I3 correlated a CONSTANT against a VARIABLE

I3 regressed the **ionisation ladder** (electron binding energies) against the grammar coordinates
n_d, n_t. But the ionisation ladder is set by how many protons the electrons see — **Z** — and to
leading order it is *identical for every isotope of an element*. The only isotopic difference is the
**ppm-scale isotope shift, which I3 did not use.** Meanwhile n_t = N − Z **ranges across the isotopes**
of any fixed element:

| element | stable isotopes | n_t range | ionisation ladder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ca | 5 (40–46) | 0–6 | one fixed ladder (Z=20) |
| Fe | 4 (54–58) | 2–6 | one fixed ladder (Z=26) |
| Sn | 10 (112–124) | 12–24 | one fixed ladder (Z=50) |
| Xe | 8 (124–134) | 16–26 | one fixed ladder (Z=54) |

Within an element the ladder is constant while n_t sweeps a wide range. **A constant cannot correlate
with a variable.** The I3 null was guaranteed by the choice of observable, independent of any physics.
It is the same class of error as an IDENTITY-PASS, inverted: not "trivially true" but **trivially null.**

## 2. The observable I3 skipped — where the coupling actually lives

The valence electron *does* read the nucleus — through the **isotope shift**: the same optical
transition sits at a slightly different frequency in each isotope, and that shift moves with N (= n_t).
This is a valence electron reporting the nuclear structure directly. It is non-zero, measured to
sub-ppm, and textbook. I3 discarded exactly this term (called it "ppm-scale") and then concluded from
its absence that there is no coupling. The coupling was in the residual it threw away.

**And the repo already holds the SDT-native half of it** — filed under the King-plot campaign, not
under NP24: the King-plot **nonlinearity tracks the mass-defect ledger shape** (the Ca repack couplet
