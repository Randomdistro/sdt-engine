THE FORK · zero free parameters on either branch02
mechanism

Gravity as mutual occlusion of the isotropic convergence flux.

The spation lattice carries convergence flux inward isotropically. A mass presents an occlusion cross-section, attenuating the flux transmitted through it.

Each mass lies in the other's occlusion shadow; the unattenuated flux on the far side delivers a net push along the line of centres.

A momentum imbalance, not an attraction. Its magnitude scales with the incident flux actually reaching the apparatus.

OCCLUSION · Law III03
the key geometry

The planet occludes the lower hemisphere — a one-sided flux field.

At the surface the planet subtends a full hemisphere; half of the incident convergence flux is occluded.

With altitude the horizon depresses by the angle

δ(h) = arccos( R / (R+h) )

Unoccluded sky is recovered and the incident flux increases, so the local coupling grows monotonically with altitude.

the instrument

A stack of five fixed-tilt torsion disks, each engaging when δ reaches its tilt.

drag = rotate · scroll = zoom · slider = climb the altitude

Five disks per stack, tilts fixed in increasing increments from horizontal. Lead test cups sit on the rims; the source masses are fixed on four rigid rods and remain stationary.

As altitude increases the horizon depression δ = arccos(R/(R+h)) grows. When δ exceeds a disk's tilt, that disk couples to its source masses and registers — sequentially, from the base upward.

Four identical stacks at 90° azimuth provide passive common-mode rejection; their slight phase offset yields a signature no linear acceleration or drift reproduces.

orientation coupling

GR couples only to the radial direction; SDT couples to the receding horizon.

Red — GR. The coupling depends only on the radial separation from the planet's centre; altitude leaves it unchanged.

Green — SDT. As the platform climbs — balloon to 40 km, then sounding rocket beyond 400 km — the horizon recedes and the tangent sightlines sweep outward along the limb. The depression δ increases throughout the ascent.

GR vs SDT · the engagement sequence is the signature09
the lock

Two independent observables — a force ratio and an angle — coincide to <1%.

ΔF/F = sin( β )

The fractional force increment (dimensionless) and the orientation budget (an angle, in radians) are measured independently.

SDT requires them equal at every altitude: ΔF/F = sin β.

No systematic raises the force by 11% while opening the orientation cone by 0.11 rad in lockstep.

THE LOCK · two observables, one curve10
acceleration drag

Reject platform acceleration mechanically, not in software.

  • Four-arm symmetry — uniform acceleration cancels: opposing arms balance to first order.
  • Gimbal & self-righting bell — any pitch raises the centre of mass against gravity, restoring vertical to ≪1°.
  • Carousel modulation — the signal is encoded at a known rotation frequency and recovered by lock-in detection.
  • Descent measurement — a slow, controlled return with the signal decreasing reproducibly.
  • Hysteresis null — each disk must disengage on descent at the altitude it engaged on ascent; the two must agree.
NOISE · passive, mechanical, self-validating11
the platform

Staged expansion rather than burst — a cascading balloon manifold.

A pinch-valve manifold: as the primary envelope reaches its elastic limit, the falling ambient pressure opens the next valve and helium fills the subsequent chamber.

h_float = H · ln( ρ₀ V / m_total )

Each stage increases the displaced volume and raises the float ceiling. Realistic terminus ≈ 50 km, where ΔF/F ≈ +12.5% and rising.

Each stage is a neutral-buoyancy station, providing dwell points at the altitudes where successive disks engage.

what decides it

Three criteria must hold jointly; otherwise the result favours GR.

engages base-upward along R(sec θ−1)

An inert stack through 40 km falsifies the prediction outright — the test is genuinely two-sided and may confirm general relativity.

FALSIFICATION CRITERIA14

A Cavendish balance carried from sea level into near-vacuum. General relativity predicts an altitude-independent coupling. Occlusion predicts a measured rise of √(2h/R).
