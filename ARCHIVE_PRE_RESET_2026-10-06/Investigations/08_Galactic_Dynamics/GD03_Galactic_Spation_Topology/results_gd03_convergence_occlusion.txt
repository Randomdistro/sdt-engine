MECHANISM:
  Baryons = displacement volumes in the spation lattice.
  They reduce convergence pressure from their direction.
  A test particle at R experiences a NET gradient differential:
    ΔP(R) = P_conv × [Ω_rim(R) - Ω_centre(R)] / 4π
  v(R) is set by the radius where ΔP produces orbital balance.
  v²(R) = c² × Ϟ_eff(R) / R


=== BULGE: SPHEROID CONVERGENCE OCCLUSION (^1.3 FACTOR) ===

   Physical basis:
   A thin DISK engine at r occludes only the equatorial convergence
   slice (2D solid angle strip: Ω_disk ≈ 2π × δθ steradians).
   A SPHEROID engine at r occludes from ALL azimuthal + polar directions
   simultaneously (full 3D: Ω_spheroid ≈ 4π × f_ellipsoid steradians).

   The solid-angle occlusion RATIO for oblate spheroid (q ≈ 0.6):
   Ω_spheroid / Ω_disk = (r/r_ref)^0.3

   This ratio is the ^1.3 factor — it tells how much MORE of the
   convergence hemisphere is occluded by a spheroid shell versus
   an equivalent disk shell at the same radius.

   Note: this does NOT change the velocity at that radius —
   the velocity is observed and fixed. It changes how much of
   that Ϟ is available as NET gradient differential to the
   interior test particle. The spheroid funnels more occlusion
   toward the centre than a disk of equal Ϟ would.

   The ^1.3 exponent is purely geometric.
   At r = r_ref (0.2 kpc, inner bulge boundary): factor = 1.0 (reference).
   At r = 3.5 kpc (bar edge): factor = 2.360.
   At the disk boundary the geometry flattens to 2D and f → const.
   The factor is the reason the bulge velocity profile is
   STEEPER than a planar disk would predict from the same Ϟ.

=== BAR: SOLID-LINE OCCLUSION STREAK ===

   The bar is a CONTINUOUS line of displacement engines.
   From any test particle's frame, it subtends a STREAK
   across the convergence hemisphere — not point occlusions.

   Key property of a solid line:
   The angular WIDTH of the streak (δφ) is CONSTANT along
   the bar's length — independent of distance along the line.
   This produces a constant occlusion fraction for all test
   particles in the bar's shadow, locking the rotation onset.

   MW Bar parameters (Wegg et al. 2015):
   Half-length: 3.5 kpc, Half-width: 0.5 kpc, Angle: 27°

   R [kpc]    streak_frac  δφ [deg]
   -------    -----------     -------
        3.5      0.250000       45.00°
        4.0      0.147584       26.57°
        4.5      0.102416       18.43°
        5.0      0.077979       14.04°
   The streak narrows monotonically beyond the bar terminus (3.5 kpc).
   At the terminus itself the full half-width is subtended (45°).
   The angular WIDTH at 3.5 kpc is maximum — this is where the streak
   has greatest effect and locks the onset of the flat rotation zone.
   The bar does not need extra mass — its geometry is sufficient.
