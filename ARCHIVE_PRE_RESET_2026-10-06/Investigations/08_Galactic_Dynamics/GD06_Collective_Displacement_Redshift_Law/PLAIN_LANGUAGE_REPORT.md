# GD06 & children — Plain-language report (RUN 1, 2026-07-13)

## What we were testing
The idea that **mass is just space with a steeper pressure gradient**, and that the gradient is set by **occlusion** — how much of the sky, at a given point, is covered by matter. Light climbing out of a covered region comes out redshifted. If that's real, one law should connect a heavy atom (its nucleus covering a sliver of the electron's sky) all the way up to a galaxy bulge and a cluster. And a sharper claim rode on top: that the redshift of a galaxy's bulge is so strong it's being *mistaken* for rotation speed.

## What the run actually found

**1. The instrument is correct — occlusion reproduces gravity, with no G and no M.** When you compute the redshift properly as "how much sky is covered along the light's escape," you get exactly the ordinary gravitational redshift (`ϟ/r`), for everything from a proton to a black hole. That's a real win of *provenance*: the same number physics already measures, but derived from geometry and sky-coverage instead of from mass and Newton's G. The Sun comes out at its known 636 m/s; that anchors the calculation.

**2. The bulge is dilute — the sharp claim does not hold.** This is the honest headline, and it goes against the hypothesis. Computed, a galaxy bulge's depth redshift is about **143 m/s** — only **0.07%** of its ~200 km/s rotation. So the bulge is *not* a dense, redshift-dominated region; it's an ordinary weak-field one. **The disk rotation is not a redshift illusion.** The regime where redshift really does masquerade as motion doesn't begin until **neutron-star density**, millions of times denser than a bulge.

**But** — the 143 m/s is real and predictable. It just hides in the wrong place to fake rotation: it cancels out of the two-sided rotation measurement and shows up instead in the bulge's *velocity spread* and in the *center being slightly more redshifted than the edge*. That's a clean, zero-guess prediction you can go check with existing data (~140 m/s), separate from rotation entirely.

**3. The cluster prediction matches the measurement.** A galaxy cluster's depth redshift comes out at **14 km/s**; the measured value (Wojtak 2011) is **~10 km/s**. Agreement, with nothing fitted. The catch: that same number is what General Relativity predicts too, so it confirms the machinery is right but doesn't yet beat GR.

**4. The one real unknown — and it's the make-or-break.** For the whole picture to be genuinely new (and genuinely constant-free, as the theory demands), the "occlusion strength" of one baryon — how much sky it effectively covers — has to come out of pure geometry. Right now it doesn't: the Sun forces a value (a proton effectively blocks about 10⁻²⁴ of its geometric area), and that number doesn't yet match the theory's other "transparency" numbers (the transfer function ~10⁻¹⁷, the per-crossing 10⁻¹⁸, the per-baryon engagement 10⁻³¹ from your solar census). Until those collapse to one angle, GD06 is a **mass-free re-telling of gravity with an unexplained coupling** — cleaner than GR, but not yet past it.

## Where each piece landed
- **GD06 (the instrument):** works, recovers gravity, mass-free. ✔
- **XSD-A (atom / King plot):** not run — needs the isotope-shift data pulled in. The atomic anchor is still owed.
- **XSD-B (the coupling):** the crux, and it's **open** — the occlusion strength isn't geometric yet. Deferred to the electropause/EMC01 work.
- **XSD-C (bulge):** **dilute, 143 m/s** — the dense-bulge idea is quantitatively ruled out; the real effect is a small, testable systematic in the bulge's spread, not its rotation.
- **XSD-D (cluster):** **14 km/s, matches ~10 km/s measured** — consistent, but tied with GR.

## The one honest sentence
Occlusion-as-sky-coverage is the right instrument and it re-derives gravity's redshift from pure geometry across forty orders of magnitude — but the bulge turned out ordinary (143 m/s, not a rotation illusion), the cluster agrees with everyone including GR, and the whole thing only becomes *new* physics if the occlusion coupling can be shown to be a pure angle. That coupling is the next brick, and it lives in the atom (XSD-A) and the electropause (XSD-B), not the galaxy.
