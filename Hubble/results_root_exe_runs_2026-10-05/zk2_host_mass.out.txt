# SN Ia Host Galaxy Mass Step — SDT Analysis

> Tests whether the 0.04 mag mass step has a
> displacement-field explanation.
> WARNING: This is the weakest test. Honest assessment.

## 1. The Observed Mass Step

```
Pantheon+ (Brout et al. 2022):

  SN Ia in hosts with log(M*/Msun) > 10 are on average
  0.040 ± 0.016 mag BRIGHTER (after standardization)
  than SN Ia in lower-mass hosts.

  This is known as the 'mass step' and is usually treated
  as a nuisance systematic in cosmological analyses.
```

## 2. Mechanism A: Velocity Dispersion (z_env)

SDT says: the host galaxy's internal displacement field
adds an extra z at the SN emission site.

z_env = (sigma_v / c)^2   (bridge law: z = v^2/c^2)

```
Host Type                  log(M*)     sig_v         z_env     k_env
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Low-mass spiral                9.5        50     2.782e-08      5996
Milky Way analogue            10.6       110     1.346e-07      2725
High-mass elliptical          11.0       200     4.451e-07      1499
Cluster elliptical            11.5       300     1.001e-06       999
```

### Predicted mass step from velocity dispersion:

```
delta_z    = z_env(high) - z_env(low)
           = 4.451e-07 - 2.782e-08
           = 4.172e-07

At z_galaxy = 0.10:
delta_mu   = (5/ln10) × delta_z / z_galaxy
           = 9.060e-06 mag

Observed:    0.040 mag
Ratio:       2.27e-04

VERDICT: Mechanism A produces a mass step that is
         4415 TIMES TOO SMALL.
         Velocity dispersion z_env does NOT explain
         the mass step.
```

## 3. Mechanism B: Galaxy Displacement Field (kappa_galaxy)

The galaxy's aggregate displacement field at the SN location:

kappa_galaxy = R_galaxy × z_galaxy_surface
z_surface = (v_circ / c)^2

```
Host Type                   v_circ     z_surface    R_eff(m)    kappa(m)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Low-mass spiral                120     1.602e-07   9.257e+19   1.483e+13
Milky Way analogue             220     5.385e-07   3.086e+20   1.662e+14
High-mass elliptical           300     1.001e-06   4.629e+20   4.635e+14
Cluster elliptical             400     1.780e-06   7.714e+20   1.373e+15
```

### Predicted mass step from galaxy kappa:

```
At SN location r = 5 kpc:
  z_extra(low-mass)  = kappa_low  / r_SN = 9.613e-08
  z_extra(high-mass) = kappa_high / r_SN = 3.004e-06
  delta_z = 2.908e-06

At z_galaxy = 0.10:
  delta_mu = 6.315e-05 mag

Observed:     0.040 mag
Ratio:        1.58e-03

VERDICT: Mechanism B also produces a mass step that is
         633 TIMES TOO SMALL.
         Galaxy displacement field at the SN location
         does NOT explain the mass step.
```

## 4. Mechanism C: Different Local κ (Environment Density)

The most natural SDT explanation:
SN Ia in massive galaxies sample denser environments
→ higher local matter density → more occlusion → lower κ → lower H₀_local.

Lower κ means photons from these SN traverse LESS compression
per unit distance → they appear CLOSER → BRIGHTER.

```
If SN Ia in low-mass hosts see H0_eff = 73.5 km/s/Mpc
and SN Ia in high-mass hosts see H0_eff = 71.5 km/s/Mpc

delta_mu = 5 log10(H0_low / H0_high)
         = 5 log10(73.5/71.5)
         = 0.0599 mag

Observed: 0.040 mag
Ratio:    1.498

Required H0 split for 0.040 mag step:
  H0_low_mass  = 73.68 km/s/Mpc
  H0_high_mass = 72.33 km/s/Mpc
  delta_H0     = 1.34 km/s/Mpc

Required kappa contrast:
  delta_kappa / kappa = 0.0183
                      = 1.83%

Is a 1.83% variation in local kappa plausible?
  H0 tension range: 67.4 to 73.0 → 7.7% variation
  Required for mass step: 1.83% variation
  This is 23.70% of the full H0 tension range.
```

**VERDICT**: Mechanism C is PLAUSIBLE. A 1.83% κ variation between
low-mass and high-mass host environments would explain
the mass step as a real environmental effect, not a
nuisance systematic.

## 5. Honest Assessment

```
Mechanism A (velocity dispersion z_env):     TOO SMALL by ~10^5×
Mechanism B (galaxy displacement field):     TOO SMALL by ~10^3×
Mechanism C (local kappa variation):         PLAUSIBLE

The mass step CANNOT be explained by direct displacement-field
z-offsets at the SN emission site (Mechanisms A and B). The
extra z is too small by many orders of magnitude.

However, Mechanism C — the same kappa-gradient mechanism that
SDT uses to explain the Hubble tension — naturally predicts
that SN Ia in denser environments (higher-mass hosts) see a
slightly different effective H0 than SN in underdense environments.

The required kappa variation (1.83%) is small compared to the full
Hubble tension range (7.7%), making it physically reasonable.

CAVEAT: This is a qualitative argument, not a quantitative
prediction. SDT does not yet provide a formula for kappa(rho_matter)
that would predict the mass step from first principles.
This is an OPEN PROBLEM.
```

   - The mapping from occlusion deficit to effective kappa

3. Direct correlation test: Hubble residuals vs host
   galaxy velocity dispersion (not just mass step).
   If SDT is correct, residuals should correlate with
   sigma_v^2 (= z_env), not just with M* alone.
