## The rule

**Triton contraction:** adding a triton draws the nuclear boundary *in* — the triton scaffold seats
tighter than the deuteron filling. Established this phase across the measured radii:

- **88%** of isotope chains: effective r0 = R/A^(1/3) falls as triton fraction f_t rises (mean corr −0.75)
- **90%** of isobar lines: at fixed A, the boundary contracts as n_t rises (median ~4 milli-fm/triton, up to ~11)
- **Shell modulation:** the contraction is *maximal at shell closures* — 3-point kink curvature −17 milli-fm
  ON a neutron closure, −25 ON a proton closure, fading to ~0 mid-shell. Every magic number complies:
  N = 20 (−6), 28 (−32), 50 (−22), 82 (−12), 126 (−4); openers relax to the smooth trend.

**Reading (Harvey):** the spation pressure makes the interleaved packing the path of least resistance;
the triton interleaves tighter than the deuteron, so the boundary contracts — hardest where a shell
closes. Nothing is held from within; the packing *is* the pressure's least-resistance solution.

**Honest bound:** because n_t = N − Z, the contraction shares its numbers with the standard isospin/
symmetry term — the grammar supplies the **mechanism** (interleaved compaction) the standard model only
parametrises. A clean native win awaits deriving the ~11 milli-fm/triton from the interleave geometry.
