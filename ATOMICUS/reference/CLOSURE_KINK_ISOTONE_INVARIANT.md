## The invariant

The compaction kink at a magic-N closure — the 3-point curvature of the measured
boundary radius along a Z-chain, in milli-fm — is a property of the **closure event**,
not of the triton occupancy. Along an isotone the kink stays constant while the
triton count n_t = N − Z varies by factors of 2 to 4:

- **N = 28** (3 members): −29.0 at 8t, −28.4 at 4t, −38.0 at 3t — constant model
  RMS 4.4 vs occupancy-proportional 15.5.
- **N = 50** (5 members): n_t spans 14 → 8; kinks −19.2 to −24.6, mean −22.2 —
  constant RMS 1.7 vs proportional 5.7.
- **N = 82** (9 members): n_t spans 30 → 16; kinks −8.9 to −13.9, mean −12.3 —
  constant RMS 1.4 vs proportional 3.2.

The constant model beats the occupancy-proportional model on every adjudicable
isotone. All 19 computable closure kinks are negative, against a non-magic
background field centred at +0.78 ± 20.05 milli-fm.

## What it means

The **freeze does the compacting; the ledger only decides who sits in the seats.**
This is the "occupancy frozen at closure" clause of the shell schedule
(`Engine/include/sdt/laws.hpp`, `sdt::laws::nuclear`) as a measured invariant
rather than narration: the closure event grips the boundary by the same amount
whether the tier's seats are filled with tritons or deuteron-side structure.

Companion result, same run: **lone rod inward** — 83.1% of odd-N members (271/326,
whole-range census) sit below the even–even midpoint of their neighbours, the
direction the seat law's filling order requires. Scope: the pairing account
predicts the same sign; shared form, cannot discriminate between accounts.

## Scope and limitations

- Adjudication engaged three isotones (N = 28, 50, 82). N = 20 and N = 126 have
  one computable member each; N = 8 has none — neighbour radii unmeasured.
- The Mn-53 outlier at N = 28 (−38.0) widens that isotone's spread; the constant
  model still wins by 3.5×.
- Post-hoc, not pre-registered: closure grip **peaks at N = 28 — the first triton
  belt** (−31.8 mean) and fades with tier size (−22.2 at 50, −12.3 at 82); the
  pre-belt N = 20 closure appears weak (−6.1, single member). Open follow-up:
  is closure grip a belt property? A pre-registered test needs a committed
  functional form before looking further.

## Relation to the triton-contraction rule

The contraction rule (`BOUNDARY_RADIUS_TRITON_CONTRACTION.md`) states the boundary
contracts as n_t rises, maximally at closures. The invariant sharpens the closure
clause: the *maximal* compaction at closure is set by the closure itself, not by
how many tritons arrived. The two are consistent — contraction tracks n_t along a
chain between closures; the kink at the closure is the event's own signature.
