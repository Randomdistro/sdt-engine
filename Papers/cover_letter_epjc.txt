Dear Editors,

I am writing to submit the manuscript entitled "An Argument for Koppa: The Introduction of a Rational Descriptor Possessing a Novel Translatable Flexibility" for consideration for publication in the European Physical Journal C.

This paper introduces the archaic Greek letter koppa (lowercase ϟ, U+03DF; uppercase Ϟ, U+03DE) as a dedicated symbol for the dimensionless kinematic ratio c/v in gravitational and atomic physics. The central result is that the observable pair (ϟ, R), where R is the measured radius of a gravitating body, is sufficient to reproduce every standard gravitational formula — orbital velocity, Keplerian period, gravitational acceleration, gravitational redshift, Schwarzschild metric, vacuum and sourced field equations, and perihelion precession — without invoking the gravitational constant G or inferred mass M at any stage.

The paper demonstrates that:

1. The koppa formulation is algebraically equivalent to the standard GM-based formulation, but replaces inputs constrained to 22 parts per million (G) with directly measured quantities constrained to below 1 part per million (v, R, c).

2. A single koppa value, ϟ = 0.5464 for the proton, governs all 126 measured ionisation energies across 21 elements (Z = 1 to 82) and 44 isoelectronic sequences, establishing that the same kinematic ratio operates identically at nuclear (10^-15 m) and stellar (10^11 m) scales — a span of over 25 orders of magnitude in length scale, and 43 orders of magnitude in the observables themselves, from a single descriptor.

3. The solar koppa, measured independently via orbital kinematics (ϟ = 686.3) and spectroscopic gravitational redshift (z = 1/ϟ^2), yields a consistent c-boundary invariant of 1,477 metres without reference to GM.

4. The Schwarzschild metric, vacuum Einstein field equation, and sourced field equation are each expressible entirely in terms of (ϟ, R), with the coupling constant 8πG/c^4 replaced by 6/(ϟ^2 c^2 R^2 ρ_bar), eliminating both G and M from the gravitational field equations.

The manuscript does not propose new physics. It proposes a notational and methodological reorganisation that makes explicit a direct scaling with c already implicit in standard theory, and advocates for its adoption through a dedicated symbol to avoid collision with the many existing uses of the letter k across physics.

I believe this work falls within the scope of EPJ-C's coverage of theoretical physics, standard model precision tests, and astroparticle physics, as it addresses the measurement foundations of gravitational dynamics and their interface with atomic structure.

This manuscript is original work, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration by any other journal. There are no conflicts of interest to declare.
