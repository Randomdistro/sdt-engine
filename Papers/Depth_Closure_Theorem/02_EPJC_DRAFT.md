## Abstract

We report an empirical identity within Spatial Displacement Theory (SDT), a
relay-lattice framework in which force is the occlusion of an isotropic
convergence throughput rather than an attraction. Defining the convergence depth
`z(r) ≡ ϟ/r` with `ϟ ≡ v²R/c²` (a velocity-defined length, numerically `GM/c²`),
we show that (i) the solar gravitational redshift equals the convergence depth at
the emission radius to 0.03 %, identifying the redshift with a real medium
property rather than a metric effect; and (ii) a single centripetal relation
`v = c√(ϟ/r) = c√z`, obtained by equating displacement-pressure to centripetal
force, reproduces orbital velocities from a 292 AU heliocentric orbit
(1.74 km/s) through Earth's orbit (29.789 km/s vs. 29.78 observed) down to the
hydrogen ground state, where it returns `k_H = c/v = 1/α = 137.036` and the
ground-state c-boundary `α²a₀ = r_e` exactly. The same depth field, read through
the movement budget `v_circ²+v²=c²`, gives the local clock rate `dτ/dt=√(1−z)`,
making `c` a position-dependent relay ceiling whose absolute value exceeds the
local terrestrial value by ≈105 m/s (the galactic depth floor). We state the
result as the **Depth–Closure Theorem**, give three falsifiable predictions
(an orbital-emitter spectral gradient, an isotropic interstellar relay-ceiling
deficit, and depth-surface clustering of Oort-cloud bodies), and discuss the
relation to, and distinctions from, general relativity. We are explicit about
scope: the identity and the centripetal law are the proven content; absolute
lattice scales carry stated caveats.

**Keywords:** gravitational redshift; equivalence principle; alternative gravity;
fine-structure constant; variable speed of light; galaxy rotation curves.

## 1. Introduction

The gravitational redshift is conventionally a consequence of metric time
dilation in a curved spacetime. We present an alternative, empirically equivalent
reading in which the redshift is a direct measurement of a medium property — the
local convergence depth of a relay lattice — and show that the same scalar field
governs bound motion across fifteen orders of magnitude in scale through one
centripetal law.

The framework is Spatial Displacement Theory (SDT): space is a nearest-neighbour
relay lattice carrying an isotropic convergence throughput; matter occludes
sectors of that throughput, and the resulting angular pressure imbalance is what
is otherwise called force. There is no attraction; bound motion is the
least-resistance path through a radial depth gradient (a closed such path is an
orbit). We make no use of `G` or of object masses `M` as primitive inputs; the
sole gravitational descriptor is `ϟ = v²R/c²`, which coincides numerically with
`GM/c²` as a derived consequence.

This paper distills a single result from that framework and tests it against data.

## 2. Definitions

For a bound configuration with circulation/orbital speed `v` at radius `r` about a
body of surface speed `v_s` and radius `R`, define the speed ratio and the
convergence depth:

```
k = c/v ,    ϟ = v_s² R / c² = R/k²  (koppa) ,    z(r) = ϟ/r = (v/c)² = 1/k².
```

`z` is dimensionless; `ϟ` has units of length but is built from velocity ratios.
Let `ℓ_P,∞`, `c_∞` denote the spation closure and relay ceiling in the
matter-free limit.

## 3. The Depth–Closure identity

We claim the following are a single quantity:

```
ℓ_P(r) = ℓ_P,∞(1 − z) ,   c_local = c_∞(1 − z) ,   dτ/dt = √(1 − z) ,   z_spectral = z_emit − z_obs .
```

Equations one to three are three readings of the closure deficit; the local relay
speed and clock rate track the local lattice closure because light advances one
spation per tick. The fourth identifies the observed spectral shift with the
depth difference between emission and observation.

### 3.1 Test against the solar gravitational redshift

At the solar photosphere the convergence depth is
`z = ϟ_⊙/R_⊙ = 2.1231×10⁻⁶` (with `ϟ_⊙ = 1477.05 m`, `R_⊙ = 6.957×10⁸ m`),
to be compared with the standard solar gravitational redshift
`GM_⊙/(c²R_⊙) = 2.1225×10⁻⁶`. The ratio is **1.0003** — agreement to 0.03 %,
limited only by the precision of the tabulated `ϟ_⊙`. Since `ϟ ≡ GM/c²`
identically, the two are the same quantity; the empirical content is the
*interpretation*: the redshift is the displacement depth at emission. Laboratory
hydrogen emits its line in the terrestrial depth regime; solar hydrogen emits in
the deeper solar regime; the recorded `z` is `z_⊙ − z_⊕`. The same construction
gives satellite clock rates as `√(1−z)` at orbital depth, i.e. the operational GPS
correction.

## 4. The single centripetal law

Equating the displacement-pressure (centripetal) acceleration to `v²/r`,

```
v²/r = g(r) = c² ϟ / r²    ⇒    v(r) = c √(ϟ/r) = c√z.
```

We test this forward, with no fitting:

| system | r | `v = c√(ϟ/r)` | reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| heliocentric orbit | 292.48 AU | 1.742 km/s | — |
| Earth orbit | 1 AU | 29.789 km/s | 29.78 km/s (obs) |
| hydrogen ground state | a₀ | v = αc | k_H = 1/α = 137.036 |

For hydrogen the law returns `z_H = α²`, hence `k_H = 1/α = 137.036` and the
ground-state c-boundary `ϟ_H = α²a₀ = 2.8179×10⁻¹⁵ m = r_e` (the classical
electron radius, here the *wake* c-boundary). Continuing inward to the proton
surface, `k = (1/α)√(R_p/a₀) = 0.5464 < 1`, a superluminal phase velocity
`1.830 c` at the proton surface, consistent with the proton residing inside its
own c-boundary. One relation spans the solar system and the atom.

## 5. Consequences

### 5.1 k as a clock; the fine-structure constant as a countdown
From the movement budget `v_circ² + v² = c²` (SDT Law V), `dτ/dt = √(1 − 1/k²)`.
As `k → 1` (`v → c`) the clock halts and bound matter dissolves at the c-boundary.
The hydrogen ground state sits `1/α = 137` steps from that boundary; `α = v/c =
1/k` is simultaneously a speed ratio, a clock rate, and a position in this
countdown — a structural reading of the fine-structure constant requiring no
appeal to coupling renormalisation.

### 5.2 The speed of light is local
The measured `c = 299 792 458 m/s` is the relay ceiling at Earth's depth. With the
galactic depth floor `z_gal = M_MW ϟ_⊙ / R_0 = 3.5×10⁻⁷`, the absolute ceiling is
`c_∞ = c/(1−z) ≈ c + 105 m/s`. The c-boundary radius is ceiling-relative, scaling
as `(1−z)²`.

### 5.3 The lumiopause
The solar depth domain terminates where the solar output, distributed over the
sphere, equals the cosmic-microwave-background flux:
`L_⊙/(4πr²) = F_CMB ⇒ r = √(L_⊙/4πF_CMB) = 20 857 AU`. This coincides with the
inner Oort distance; we suggest loosely-bound bodies rest at this depth surface
("lumiostasis").

## 6. Independent corroboration

Within the same framework and the derived acceleration floor
`a₀ = cH₀/2π = 1.042×10⁻¹⁰ m/s²`: (i) galaxy rotation curves for 135 SPARC
galaxies are reproduced at 23.8 % RMS, unbiased, with a baryonic Tully–Fisher
slope of 3.58, using only baryonic input and using the MOND 'simple'
interpolation form for the crossover (the asymptotes are SDT-derived; the
crossover shape is borrowed and its native derivation is open); (ii) the
galactic, atomic, and stellar convergence response collapse onto a single curve
in `ξ = g_self/a₀` at 21.5 % RMS across the atomic and stellar scales (the
galactic pilot is void pending a data-path repair); substituting a deliberately
incorrect floor degrades the collapse by a factor 2.4 — consistent with, though
not yet establishing, a required floor. These are reported in full in the
supplementary investigations and summarised here as consistency evidence.

## 7. Falsifiable predictions

- **A spectral gradient from an orbital emitter.** A calibrated atomic-line
  emitter on a solar-descent trajectory should show `Δλ/λ = ϟ_⊙(1/r − 1/r_obs)`,
  reaching **+2.05×10⁻⁷ at 0.046 AU**. The signal class is pre-confirmed by the
  GPS gravitational-redshift correction; the gradient is 5–6 orders above
  modern frequency-comb precision.
- **An isotropic interstellar relay-ceiling deficit** of ≈105 m/s below `c_∞`
  (the galactic depth floor), present in field-free interstellar space — absent in
  metric gravity.
- **Depth-surface clustering** of detached/Oort bodies near 20 860 AU, deviating
  from a purely Keplerian semi-major-axis distribution.

## 8. Relation to general relativity

To first order the depth law reproduces the GR weak-field results it is tested
against (redshift, Shapiro delay, perihelion precession), because `ϟ ≡ GM/c²`. The
distinctions are mechanistic and, at second order, observational: SDT predicts a
position-dependent relay ceiling with an absolute offset (§5.2, §7) that has no
metric counterpart, and attributes bound motion to a pushing convergence gradient
rather than to geodesics in curvature. The present paper does not claim to
supersede GR; it claims an empirically equivalent first-order reading with
distinct, testable second-order content.

## 9. Scope and limitations

The proven content is the redshift–depth identity (§3.1, 0.03 %) and the single
centripetal law (§4, forward-validated). The following carry explicit caveats and
are stated as future work, not as results: the *absolute* spation closure
`ℓ_P,∞` requires one irreducible scale-seed (a dimensional necessity; only its
*variation* `1−z` is framework-native); the galactic-floor magnitude inherits the
Milky-Way mass uncertainty (~±20 %); and the internal proton/electron radii used
in §4–5 are measured, not yet derived. None of these enters the theorem's proven
row.

## 10. Conclusion

Within SDT, the gravitational redshift is the convergence depth at the emission
point, and one centripetal law `v = c√z` governs bound motion from the atom to
the outer solar system, returning `1/α = 137` and `r_e` at the hydrogen scale
without adjustable parameters (the hydrogen closure returns α by construction).
We have stated the result as a theorem, tested its
core to 0.03 %, and given three falsifiable predictions that distinguish it from
metric gravity. The reproducibility script accompanying this paper regenerates
every numerical claim in a single run.
