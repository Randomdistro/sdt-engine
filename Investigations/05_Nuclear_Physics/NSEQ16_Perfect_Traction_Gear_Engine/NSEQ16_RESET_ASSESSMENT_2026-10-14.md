## 12. Identical-angle traction on the wound ring (Harvey, 2026-10-16; registered before `nseq16_traction_1.py` exists)
**Question put by Harvey:** NSEQ19 used hard spheres; the nucleons are wound multi-tour toruses. Did the identical-angle traction notion (two high-velocity meshes mesh when at the same position on the minor circumference and the same direction of toroidal rotation) check out?
**Record, stated plainly.** It was not tested. NSEQ19 is a sphere stacker (registration section 4: "spheres, not filament bundles; static geometry; no flow, no meshing phase"). NSEQ16 section 8 ran rings, but static, parallel-axis, with a non-overlap test only. The engine's slip gate is identically zero for unlike pairs (section 12 of NSEQ18). No velocity-matching test of the notion exists before this section.

**Model.** The engine's H1 curve K(u; ρ): radial coordinate R + a cos φ, azimuth θ = 4u, height a sin φ, φ = 3u + ρ (4 tours, 3 cross-section turns; R = 0.99874, a = 0.05042 in R_p). Material flows along the curve at constant speed s, so the surface velocity at a curve point is s times the unit tangent τ = (−3a sin φ, 4(R + a cos φ), 3a cos φ) ÷ |…| in (radial, azimuthal, vertical) components. Thin-filament limit: two rings in contact touch at the outermost strand point of each ring at the facing azimuth (the strand of the four at that azimuth with the largest R + a cos φ, i.e. |φ_out| ≤ π/4). Traction (no slip) at the contact requires equal velocity vectors: slip = |τ_A − τ_B| (0 = mesh, 2 = counter-sliding).
**Pair families** (B placed to face A along the line of centres): F1 same chirality, same sense of circulation (co-rotating, like units); F2 same chirality, reversed circulation; F3 mirror image across the contact plane (chirality flipped, circulation reversed: the p/n complementary rotation of NP30 section 5); F4 mirror across the ring plane (chirality flipped, circulation kept); F5 F4 with reversed circulation.
