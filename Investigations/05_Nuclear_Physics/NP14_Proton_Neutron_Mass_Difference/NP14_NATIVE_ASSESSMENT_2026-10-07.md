## 1. The five questions
**1. What do we not know?** What "heavier" measures natively. In SDT mass is the throughput reorganisation cost of a body (Law IV: m ∝ V_disp, with m measured as a baryon count N_b = M/m_p); the electron-volt is a unit bridge applied to movement of substance, not a substance. The neutron's excess is therefore a dimensionless ratio of resistances (m_n/m_p − 1, and the excess in units of the electron's resistance), and the open question is whether that excess, and the deuteron's apparent deficit, have a native description at all, or whether the list of constituents {p, n, e} with listed masses is not an additive ledger.
**2. Why does it matter?** The bookkeeping as it stands does not close. The deuteron's gap cannot be computed from a neutron, an electron and a proton carried as listed masses; the gap changes with the component list (against p + n it is one number, against p + p + e another). If the ledger is not additive, the method, the subject and the metrics are all in question; this is an investigation into what is at play. It matters because the current account is wrong or incomplete, in the sense that it cannot be closed from its own parts.
**3. By what methods can we reach a better understanding of the problem?** (M1) Restate the ledger in unit-free resistances and show which statements survive a change of measurement standard. (M2) Recount the light nuclei by component lists and classify each relation as construction or as content. (M3) Write the Standard Model's own account as a ledger of inputs and residuals, from sources. (M4) State a native candidate from the engine's laws with its prediction fixed first, and test it where it can fail. (M5) State what a native mechanism must supply, and the observables that would separate the accounts, independent of any numerical closure.
**4. What would prove us wrong?** (a) *Failure-pathway indicators*: F1 a unit conversion or standard carries the result; F2 agreement appears only after the measured value is loaded or after a factor is chosen; F3 many candidate forms fit equally well; F4 a prohibited input (quark or field decomposition, a wavefunction, a fitted factor) is needed; F5 the result changes when the measurement standard changes; F6 the answer changes with the component list and no mechanism says why. (b) *Falsifying mathematics*: unit invariance of every ledger statement; exactness of the identities claimed as constructions; a sign criterion on the native candidate; the closed-form consequences of the component list. (c) *What it breaks, and how to tell regardless of closure*: the consumers of the deuteron binding value in the repository (NP09, NP10, NP08, NP04A, NP04B, APS12, the benchmark suite) compare against a listed-mass binding; if the neutron is a proton with an internal electron, their target changes, and the grammar of ATOMICUS and NP17 rests on that structure; the Standard Model statement of lepton-number conservation in β decay is touched, because the electron is then a constituent released and not created. Independent observables: the deuteron photodisintegration channel into p + p + e⁻ (threshold in §4), the mirror-pair ledger over many A, and the neutron's boundary radius against the proton's.
**5. How will we know we are done?** When (a) the ledger statements are all classified and unit-free, (b) the native candidate and the mechanism requirements have been tested or specified to the precision their prediction allows, (c) every failure-pathway indicator has a written outcome, the falsifiers have been run and survived or the candidate recorded as excluded, and (d) the chain position is stated: suppliers and dependents consistent, nothing downstream silently using the wrong list, and what remains open names the supplier that would settle it. Otherwise the investigation is recorded as open, with the unmet items listed.
