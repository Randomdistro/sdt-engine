## 2. What Einstein says about the medium (Source A, verbatim)
**The medium kept, mechanically stripped.**
- "He achieved this, the most important advance in the theory of electricity since Maxwell, by taking from ether its mechanical, and from matter its electromagnetic qualities." (Einstein on what Lorentz did.)
- "According to Lorentz the elementary particles of matter alone are capable of carrying out movements; their electromagnetic activity is entirely confined to the carrying of electric charges." (A statement of the Lorentz position; quoted, not adopted here.)
- "As to the mechanical nature of the Lorentzian ether, it may be said of it, in a somewhat playful spirit, that immobility is the only mechanical property of which it has not been deprived by H. A. Lorentz."
- "...the whole change in the conception of the ether which the special theory of relativity brought about, consisted in taking away from the ether its last mechanical quality, namely, its immobility."

**Why the stationary medium was dropped (a reason about symmetry, not about absence).**
- "For the theoretician such an asymmetry in the theoretical structure, with no corresponding asymmetry in the system of experience, is intolerable."
- "More careful reflection teaches us, however, that the special theory of relativity does not compel us to deny ether. We may assume the existence of an ether; only we must give up ascribing a definite state of motion to it, i.e. we must by abstraction take from it the last mechanical characteristic which Lorentz had still left it."

**The case for a medium, from mechanics.**
- "To deny the ether is ultimately to assume that empty space has no physical qualities whatever. The fundamental facts of mechanics do not harmonize with this view."
- "Newton might no less well have called his absolute space 'Ether'; what is essential is merely that besides observable objects, another thing, which is not perceptible, must be looked upon as real, to enable acceleration or rotation to be looked upon as something real."

**Inertia and the medium (Mach).**
- "But inertial resistance opposed to relative acceleration of distant masses presupposes action at a distance; and as the modern physicist does not believe that he may accept this action at a distance, he comes back once more, if he follows Mach, to the ether, which has to serve as medium for the effects of inertia."
- "Mach's ether not only conditions the behaviour of inert masses, but is also conditioned in its state by them."
- "Mach's idea finds its full development in the ether of the general theory of relativity. According to this theory the metrical qualities of the continuum of space-time differ in the environment of different points of space-time, and are partly conditioned by the matter existing outside of the territory under consideration."

**The medium of the general theory.**
- "The ether of the general theory of relativity is a medium which is itself devoid of all mechanical and kinematical qualities, but helps to determine mechanical (and electromagnetic) events."
- "...the state of the former is at every place determined by connections with the matter and the state of the ether in neighbouring places, which are amenable to law in the form of differential equations; whereas the state of the Lorentzian ether in the absence of electromagnetic fields is conditioned by nothing outside itself, and is everywhere the same."
- "We know that it determines the metrical relations in the space-time continuum, e.g. the configurational possibilities of solid bodies as well as the gravitational fields; but we do not know whether it has an essential share in the structure of the electrical elementary particles constituting matter."
- "There can be no space nor any part of space without gravitational potentials; for these confer upon space its metrical qualities, without which it cannot be imagined at all."

**The closing statement.**
- "Recapitulating, we may say that according to the general theory of relativity space is endowed with physical qualities; in this sense, therefore, there exists an ether. According to the general theory of relativity space without ether is unthinkable; for in such space there not only would be no propagation of light, but also no possibility of existence for standards of space and time (measuring-rods and clocks), nor therefore any space-time intervals in the physical sense. But this ether may not be thought of as endowed with the quality characteristic of ponderable media, as consisting of parts which may be tracked through time. The idea of motion may not be applied to it."
- On a medium without trackable parts: "If the existence of such floats for tracking the motion of the particles of a fluid were a fundamental impossibility in physics if, in fact, nothing else whatever were observable than the shape of the space occupied by the water as it varies in time, we should have no ground for the assumption that water consists of movable particles. But all the same we could characterize it as a medium."

## 4. What the sources support, and what they do not
**They support:**
- Lorentz, in the paper that introduced the velocity-dependent masses, set all mass as an effect of the particle's field in the medium: "no other, no 'true' or 'material' mass". The resistance to acceleration, with its direction dependence, was a property of the particle moving in the medium, not of the particle alone.
- Einstein described the Lorentz step himself in 1920 as the removal of mechanical qualities from the medium and the assignment of electromagnetic qualities to matter.
- In 1920 Einstein said that rods and clocks cannot exist without the medium, that inertial effects are conditioned by the medium's state (Mach's idea), that the medium is not to be denied, that it has physical qualities, and that it need not consist of trackable parts (the float and water-surface argument).
- Einstein left open whether the medium "has an essential share in the structure of the electrical elementary particles constituting matter".

**They do not support (and the SDT picture differs here):**
- Einstein did not say that mass is the medium's resistance to altering the phase of the convergence of a throughput; this is SDT's statement (Law IV) and is not in the sources.
- Einstein's 1920 medium has no state of motion and no kinematical qualities ("The idea of motion may not be applied to it"). The SDT medium has a relay throughput and a rest frame in which the convergence is isotropic. This is a disagreement of premise, to be registered, not smoothed over.
- Einstein did not hold that mass goes to infinity at c as a property of the body. In 1905 he gave the longitudinal and transverse mass formulas (they diverge at v → c) and cautioned that their values depend on the definition of force and acceleration. In 1948 (secondary source D) he advised against the velocity-dependent mass: one tool return gave "it is not proper to speak of the mass M = m(1 − v²/c²)^(−1/2) of a moving body, because no clear definition can be given for M"; another gave "It is not good to introduce the concept of the mass M = m/√(1−v²/c²) of a moving body for which no clear definition can be given. It is better to introduce no other mass concept than the 'rest mass' m." (two English renderings of one letter; the wording needs the original). The divergence at c then stands in the energy and momentum (E = γmc²), while the "rest mass" is held invariant. So in Einstein's later account the object carries an invariant mass and the divergent resistance is kinematic.
- A caution on the 1905 transverse mass: m/(1−v²/c²) is γ²m, while the modern transverse mass is γm; the difference is a difference of definition of force (Einstein's caution quoted above).

## 5. Observations (inferences of mine, marked)
1. [Inference] The place where the medium's resistance became "the object's" is the step from Lorentz's "no true or material mass" (all mass from the particle's field in the medium) to Einstein's "rest mass" with a kinematic energy-momentum. Einstein's 1920 text, read against his 1905 text, attributes the earlier step to Lorentz's reduction of the medium to immobility and then to nothing mechanical.
2. [Inference] Einstein's stated ground for dropping the rest-state medium was the absence of an observable asymmetry. The 1920 sentence ("no corresponding asymmetry in the system of experience") makes the argument depend on that absence. Whether the cosmic microwave background's rest frame (a measured dipole) is such an asymmetry in experience is a question for SDT to register with its own criterion; it is not Einstein's statement.
3. [Inference] The float argument (a medium characterised without trackable parts) matches a relay medium in which a disturbance is passed on without any part being followed. It is a point where Einstein's 1920 text allows the SDT description, which does not need parts tracked through time.
4. [Inference] Lorentz's m₁ and m₂ already have the direction dependence (longitudinal against transverse) that SDT should reproduce from the phase of convergence of the throughput, with the ratio of the two resistances fixed by the relay geometry.
