# GOM42 P1 — Sun–Jupiter–Saturn great inequality with native inputs: registration (2026-10-14)

Written after the sources were cached (`Datasets/solar_system/GOM42_P1_SOURCES_2026-10-14.md`) and **before** `gom42_p1_1.cpp` exists. Continues `GOM42_RESET_ASSESSMENT_2026-10-14.md` (V3′ met).

## 1. Inputs (all sourced; no G, GM, M or softening)
- κ_Sun = 1476.351201 m (GOM26 orbital invariant of this programme, from the Earth–Moon barycentre arc).
- κ_Jupiter and κ_Saturn from their satellites' orbits by v²r = c²κ in the Kepler form κ = 4π²a³/(c²P²): Io (a = 421 800 km, P = 1.762732 d) and Titan (a = 1 221 900 km, P = 15.945448 d) from the JPL SSD satellite table. (The mean-element period of the table is used as listed; any difference from a purely Keplerian period of the real orbit, for example from the planet's oblateness, is carried as a stated input systematic.)
- Initial state: Standish Table 2a elements of Jupiter and Saturn at J2000 (a, e, I, L, ϖ, Ω), the heliocentric Keplerian state with μ = c²(κ_Sun + κ_planet), then shifted to the κ-weighted barycentre. AU = 149 597 870 700 m (IAU, exact).
- Bodies: Sun, Jupiter, Saturn. Mercury–Mars, Uranus and Neptune are omitted (stated model limitation).

## 2. Observable and gate (committed now)
- Integrator: the validated velocity Verlet of `gom42_reset_1.cpp` (a_i = c² Σ κ_j (r_j − r_i)/r³), Δt = 2 days, T = 6000 yr from J2000 forward.
- Osculating heliocentric elements of each planet every 30 days; the osculating mean longitude λ = Ω + ω + M, unwrapped.
- Remove short periods by a boxcar of 59.58 yr (3 Jupiter–Saturn synodic periods, ≈ 5 Jupiter and 2 Saturn orbits). Residual: λ(t) after removing a linear plus quadratic polynomial. The dominant long-period sinusoid: least-squares fit with the period scanned from 400 to 2000 yr in steps of 1 yr (then refined in steps of 0.05 yr around the best); amplitude and phase fitted.
- **Gate G-P1:** the fitted period of the dominant long-period term in Jupiter's λ residual lies within **2%** of the sourced 938.68 yr, i.e. in [920.9, 957.5] yr.
- **Reported, not gated:** the amplitudes in Jupiter's and Saturn's λ (sourced 0.3615° and 0.8835°); the ratio of the Saturn to Jupiter amplitudes (sourced 2.44); the Saturn period (same sinusoid scan on Saturn's λ).
- **Sensitivity (reported):** the same run with κ_Sun raised by 1.9×10⁻⁴ (the GM-equivalent offset recorded in GOM26/GOM27: ϟ_Sun/ (GM_Sun/c²) − 1 = −1.85×10⁻⁴) and with κ_Jupiter, κ_Saturn changed by ±0.8% (the Kepler-form mismatch of the satellite periods).
- **Label:** convergent with Newtonian N-body at this order (same 1/r² law): the test is of the native κ inputs and of the integrator, not a discriminator (the prompt's own statement).

## 3. Validations (before the physics run)
- **VP1:** single-planet Kepler orbit (Sun + Jupiter only) over 600 yr: the osculating a and e stay constant to 1×10⁻⁸ relative and λ advances linearly (residual after the linear fit < 1×10⁻⁶ rad).
- **VP2:** elements → state → elements round trip for the Standish initial state: relative error in a, e < 1×10⁻¹² and angles < 1×10⁻¹² rad.
- **VP3:** the period scan recovers a planted 938.68 yr sinusoid (amplitude 0.36°) on the same sample grid with white noise 0.01° to within 0.5 yr.
- **VP4:** invariants over the run: normalised momentum drift ≤ 1×10⁻¹¹, angular momentum ≤ 1×10⁻¹⁰, relative energy drift ≤ 1×10⁻⁸.

## 4. Hand estimates (before running)
κ_Jupiter from Io = 1.4097 m × (1.7627/1.7691)^(−2) ≈ 1.42 m (+0.8% from the true GM_J/c²). The mean-motion combination 2n_J − 5n_S = −40.77°/cy gives a free-resonance period of 883 yr; the Standish f = 38.35°/cy (period 938.7 yr) includes the perihelion rates through the mean anomalies (2M_J − 5M_S rate = −38.42°/cy, 0.2% from f). The physical critical argument of the near-resonance is the third-order combination 2λ_J − 5λ_S + 3ϖ with 3ϖ-type terms of 0.5–1.6°/cy, so a long-period term near 39–40°/cy, i.e. a period of **about 900–920 yr**, is expected from a faithful N-body: that sits 2–4% below 938.7 and may fail the 2% gate by definition rather than by the native inputs. The sensitivity to κ_Sun is amplified by ≈ (n_J/ (2n_J − 5n_S)) ≈ 75: a −1.85×10⁻⁴ offset moves the period by about 1.4%. Amplitudes: Jupiter ≈ 0.3° and Saturn ≈ 0.8° (within the omitted-planet limitation, ≈ 10%).

## 5. Addendum before the run (written before `gom42_p1_1.cpp` exists)
(a) **Initial mean anomaly.** Standish's note to Table 2a/2b says the computation of M for Jupiter through Neptune must include the Table 2b terms; at J2000 (T = 0) that gives M₀ = L − ϖ + c (cos 0 = 1, sin 0 = 0), i.e. +0.06064° for Jupiter and −0.13434° for Saturn. M₀ is taken as the source prescribes. (b) **VP1 gate corrected before any run.** A velocity-Verlet orbit has osculating-element oscillations of order (n Δt)²: for Jupiter with Δt = 2 d, (n Δt)² = 8×10⁻⁶, so the registered 1×10⁻⁸ constancy of a and e in VP1 cannot be met by any second-order integrator and is a registration error of mine. VP1 is now: the osculating a and e stay within 3 (n Δt)² of their initial values with no secular drift (the linear-fit change over 600 yr below 1×10⁻⁷ relative), and the residual of λ after the linear fit stays below 1×10⁻⁴ rad. (c) VP3 (planted recovery), VP2, VP4 and the gate G-P1 are unchanged.

## 6. Attempt 1 and Amendment 1 (instrument defects; registered before attempt 2)
**Attempt 1 (kept: `gom42_p1_1_results.txt`, `gom42_p1_1_attempt1_diagnostic_spectrum.txt`).** VP1–VP3 met; **VP4 not met** (energy 3.6×10⁻⁷ against the registered 1×10⁻⁸; momentum and angular momentum 10⁻¹⁴); the main run gave the dominant period **473.15 yr (−49.6%)** with Jupiter amplitude 0.0748° (sourced 0.3615°), Saturn 473.15 yr, 0.1856° (ratio 2.48; sourced 2.44); G-P1 NOT MET. A post-seen diagnostic spectrum shows a single peak at 473 yr and amplitudes ≤ 0.004° between 880 and 960 yr.
**Defects found before reading any of this as physics.** (i) The least-squares normal equations used unscaled monomials in t (t up to 6000, y up to 1.8×10⁵ deg): the 5×5 system is ill-conditioned, and the amplitude floor in the spectrum (~0.003°) is of the order of its round-off. VP3 did not test this because its planted series had a negligible trend, a deficiency of my validation. (ii) The VP4 energy limit of 1×10⁻⁸ is unattainable for a second-order symplectic step (the energy oscillates at about (n Δt)² times a body-mass fraction), the same kind of error as VP1's in the addendum.
**Amendment 1.** The polynomial terms use scaled time u = (t − 3000)/3000 (the model is unchanged); VP3's planted series now carries the realistic trend 30.349037 °/yr × (t − 3000) + 2×10⁻⁴ (t − 3000)² before the planted sinusoid and noise, and must still recover the period to 0.5 yr and the amplitude to 5%; VP4's energy limit is replaced by: the energy deviation stays below 2 (n_J Δt)² = 1.7×10⁻⁵ with no secular growth (the maximum over the second half of the run not larger than 1.5 times the maximum over the first half). The gate G-P1, the sensitivities and everything else are unchanged. Attempt 2 reports whether the 473-yr peak survives.

## 7. Attempt 2 outcome under the registered initial conditions, diagnosis, and Amendment 2 (POST-SEEN)
**Attempt 2 (kept: `gom42_p1_1_results_attempt2_registered_ICs.txt`).** With the scaled fit and the realistic VP3 (recovered 938.65 yr and 0.360°, met) the main run is unchanged: Jupiter's dominant period **473.15 yr** (−49.59%), amplitude 0.0748°; Saturn the same period, 0.1856° (ratio 2.48; sourced 2.44); **G-P1 NOT MET** under the registered initial conditions. VP4 as amended: the energy deviation 3.6×10⁻⁷ is below 2 (n_J Δt)² = 1.7×10⁻⁵ (met); the "no secular growth" half was not evaluated (energy sampled every 20 000 steps, only the maximum kept).
**Diagnosis (post-seen diagnostics, kept: `gom42_p1_1_attempt2_diagnostics_mean_motions.txt`).** (i) With both planetary masses ×10⁻⁶ the simulated mean longitude rates equal the Kepler mean motions to 3×10⁻⁶ (J) and 5×10⁻⁷ (S): the integrator and the element conversions are sound. (ii) With the real κ's the simulated mean rates over 6000 yr are λ̇_J = 3036.098 and λ̇_S = 1230.104 deg/cy against Standish's L̇ = 3034.904 and 1222.115: **+0.039% and +0.65%**. (iii) The resonance angle 2λ_J − 5λ_S therefore has a mean rate of **−78.32 deg/cy** (period 459.6 yr) in the simulation against −40.77 deg/cy (883 yr) for the sourced L̇: the near-commensurability is a difference of two nearly equal numbers (6069.8 − 6110.6), so a 0.65% error in Saturn's rate doubles it. (iv) The 473-yr line is the resonance angle's own period seen in λ_J, a_J and a_S (a_S amplitude 1.3×10⁻³ AU at 473 yr), i.e. the physical Jupiter–Saturn mutual term with the wrong resonance proximity; the Saturn/Jupiter amplitude ratio 2.48 equals the expected angular-momentum ratio. (v) Cause: the registered rule "Standish's mean semi-major axes are the osculating ones at J2000" does not reproduce Standish's mean motions in a dynamical integration (Saturn's mean longitude rate is raised by Jupiter's field by 0.7% and the osculating a of the real planet is not its mean a). This is a defect of the initial-condition rule, not of the native κ inputs: the κ_Sun sensitivity run changed the period by 0.00 yr and the κ_J, κ_S ×0.992 run by 1.85 yr, whereas the initial a changes it by a factor two.
**Amendment 2 (post-seen; the first outcome above stays recorded as the registered result).** Initial conditions are data: the osculating semi-major axes at J2000 are set so that the simulated mean longitude rates over the 6000-yr run equal the sourced Standish L̇ (λ̇_J = 3034.90371757, λ̇_S = 1222.11494724 deg/cy) to 1×10⁻⁶ relative, by a secant iteration on the two scale factors s_J, s_S multiplying Standish's a (angles, e, I, ϖ, Ω, M₀ unchanged). The observed mean motions are thus used as the initial condition; **no great-inequality quantity (period, amplitude) enters the calibration**; the two scale factors are reported (CALIBRATED(2), to the sourced L̇, label attached). The gate G-P1 (period within 2% of 938.68 yr) and the sensitivities are unchanged and applied to the calibrated run. The hand estimate for this run is the free-resonance period of the sourced mean motions corrected by the perihelion terms: 883–946 yr; if it falls outside 920.9–957.5 yr the gate fails again and that is recorded.

## 8. Amendment 2 outcome (kept: `gom42_p1_1_results_attempt3_with_amendment2.txt`)
The secant iteration on the two scale factors did **not converge** in 14 iterations for any variant: after the last update the simulated mean rates were off the sourced L̇ by −3.4×10⁻⁵ (J) and **−1.6×10⁻³ (S)** in the baseline (scales s_J = 0.999838, s_S = 0.999908) and by 10⁻³ to 10⁻⁴ in the others; the dominant period moved between 477 and 1996 yr and the amplitudes between 0.076° and 2.9° (Jupiter) as the iteration passed close to the exact commensurability (period → ∞, amplitude → large). The variants are therefore not interpretable, and **no value from Amendment 2 is a result**. Why: (i) the mean rate of an unwrapped osculating longitude over a forward-only 6000-yr span is not a well-defined, smooth function of the initial a when the resonance period (≈ 0.5–2 kyr) is comparable with the span; (ii) Standish's L̇ is the linear coefficient of a fit that carries the great-inequality term separately, over a span symmetric about J2000; the comparison needs the same decomposition (a joint fit of a linear term and the long-period sinusoid over −3000 … +3000 yr, forward and backward integration), which was not registered and was not done.
**Standing result of P1.** Under the registered initial conditions: dominant period 473.15 yr against the sourced 938.68 yr (−49.6%), **G-P1 NOT MET**; Amendment 2 inconclusive. The failure traces to the initial conditions' mean motions (the resonance proximity 2n_J − 5n_S amplifies a 0.65% error in Saturn's rate to a factor 1.9 in the resonance frequency), not to the native κ inputs (the κ_Sun offset 1.85×10⁻⁴ moved the period by 0.00 yr; κ_J, κ_S ×0.992 by 1.85 yr, 0.4%). The test therefore does not decide anything about the native κ's: a faithful test needs mean motions accurate to better than about 10⁻⁵ at the start, and a symmetric decomposition as in Standish's fit. Registered successor (to register before running): ±3000-yr integration about J2000 with a joint linear + quadratic + sinusoid fit, calibrating on the linear coefficient only.
