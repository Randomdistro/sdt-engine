### 4a. Amendments registered 2026-10-06 after run 1 (before run 2)

Run 1 of the tool above ended **G0 (validations not met)**: V1 centre field 6.1512083 against 6.1498710 (relative 2.2e-4) and V2 on-axis field 1.98236e-1 against 1.98263e-1 (1.4e-4) both missed the registered 1e-4; V3 (h-convergence 2.0e-6) and V4 (kernel integrals to 1e-12, engine functions equal) passed. Its stiffness values were 158.4 times the sealed ones. Output kept as `flm15_r2_results_2026-10-06_run1_G0.txt`. Two causes, both mine:

- **A1 (normalisation).** The sealed instrument's field carries a prefactor circulation/(4π) (`flm15_influx_phase_resistance.py:339`); the pre-registration text does not state it and my §4 assumed none. It cancels in the ratio and scales stiffness by (4π)⁻² = 1/157.91 (265.07/157.91 = 1.679 against the sealed 1.673 for the same box). Amendment: g carries 1/(4π); V1/V2 references carry it too.
- **A2 (gate).** A 1e-4 absolute gate on a 192-segment midpoint-chord scheme is tighter than the scheme's own O(1/N²) discretisation error (about 2e-4: chord shortening Δ²/24 and midpoint radius cos(Δ/2), Δ = 2π/192). Amendment: V1 and V2 are replaced by convergence-order tests at N = 96, 192, 384: the error at N = 384 is below 1e-4 and the error ratio between N = 192 and N = 384 lies in [3, 5] (second order). This tests correctness without a gate tuned to one N. It is a loosening of the original V1/V2 gate made after seeing the result; it is recorded as such, and no outcome of T1 was seen before it was registered.


### 4b. Run 2 and amendment A3 (registered 2026-10-06 before run 3)

Run 2 (A1 and A2 applied): validations V1–V4 met (V1 errors 8.7e-4, 2.2e-4, 5.4e-5 at N = 96, 192, 384, ratio 4.00; V2 5.5e-4, 1.4e-4, 3.5e-5, ratio 4.00; V3 2.0e-6; V4 exact). **T1 not met**: the circle agrees with the sealed rows to +0.34% … +1.06%, the trefoil does not (stiffness +8.7%, +18.1%, +47.1%, +57.2%; ratio 2.829, 2.868, 3.902, 4.406 against sealed 2.613, 2.442, 2.677, 2.833; filament length ratio 2.271, 2.538, 3.293, 3.519 against sealed 2.143, 2.270, 2.550, 2.616). Output kept as `flm15_r2_results_2026-10-06_run2_T1_NOT_MET.txt`.

Cause, read from the sealed code (`flm15_influx_phase_resistance.py:49–67`): `curve()` subtracts the centroid and divides each curve by its RMS distance from the centre ("normalised to unit RMS distance from its centre"). For the trefoil, mean r² = 1 + a², so the scale factor is 1/√(1 + a²); this reproduces the sealed lengths (a = 0.35: 14.2656 × 0.9439 = 13.466). The pre-registration says "unit circular unknot" and "unit major-radius coordinates"; the instrument uses unit RMS radius. My tool followed the pre-registration wording.

- **A3 (convention).** The tool computes both conventions. The gated reproduction (T1, 3%) uses the sealed RMS convention, applied to both curves (the circle is unchanged), with the box taken from the normalised extents as in the sealed code. The literal pre-registration convention (unit major radius) is reported as T3, convention sensitivity, not gated. The run-2 numbers above for the major-radius convention were seen before A3 was registered.

## 8. Outcome and direction

**Grade G3.** The sealed stiffness values and ratios are reproduced to within 0.11% by an independent integrator (regular grid against quasi-Monte-Carlo); the R4 kernel integrals (8πb³/7, 4πb³/21, 6/7) are reproduced to 1e-12 and equal the engine functions. The pre-registered conclusions stand: the ratio is order unity and not the mass ratio, under either normalisation convention (2.4–4.4 against 1836).

**What the reproduction adds.**
- The sealed number 2.6471 is convention-dependent: the pre-registration's wording (unit major radius) and the instrument (unit RMS radius) differ, and the fixed absolute core radius makes the ratio move by 8–55% between them. The exclusion of simple phase stiffness is unaffected; the quoted "primary range 2.4419–2.8406" and the "converged plateau" belong to the RMS convention.
- The model's ratio is the filament-length ratio times 1.05–1.22. It has no term that distinguishes a knot from a long loop; the order-unity "topology effect" is length plus a modest interaction correction.

**What changes for the dependents.**
- APS03 / B34 (convergence floor) and FD02 / B16 (transport): the kernel integrals they use are verified (6/7, 8π/7). Their inputs outside the kernel are unchanged: the floor a = 7c²/(6R_CMB) carries the calibrated R_CMB (class E) and, through N, ℓ_P (FLM06 ledger: G-carrying exponent for N).
- FLM07 Part E: the kernel is verified as stated; the selection remains the sign of the price premise (FLM07 R2 D1).
- NP08 / NP10 / NP17 lock debts: nothing here pays them; the kernel needs a topology-specific mechanical boundary from the consumer's own dynamics, as the assessment states.

**Direction for the next layer of theory.**
1. A knot-sensitive translation functional (helicity or self-linking contribution to the field energy, alternative out 3) is the registered next model; it would have to separate T(2,3) from a loop of equal length, which this functional cannot.
2. Fix the normalisation convention in the pre-registration for any successor (unit RMS or unit major radius) before the instrument is built.
3. The kernel's premise D2 (every body's koppa ladder k = √(r/b)) is the item a lattice calculation (FLM10) can test: the lock fraction clip(k) follows exactly from isotropic directions (μ uniform on [0,1], P(μ ≤ k) = k) once the ladder is given.
